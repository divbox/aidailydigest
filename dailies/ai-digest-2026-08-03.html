Latest Open Artifacts (#23): Kimi K3, Inkling & a Frontier That Keeps Getting Crowded
Interconnects

Interconnects' latest open-model roundup opens with a contrarian observation: consolidation among AI labs, widely predicted as training costs balloon, isn't happening. Instead more organizations are training strong models and releasing them openly — Thinking Machines is the poster child, now pulling in hundreds of millions in revenue from its open-model fine-tuning service (Tinker) while shipping open weights ahead of NVIDIA's Nemotron and Arcee's Trilogy.

The headline releases: Kimi K3 from Moonshot, a ~2.8 trillion parameter MoE and one of the largest open-weight drops in some time, released under a noncommercial license that requires inference and fine-tuning providers to sign a commercial agreement — a structure Interconnects notes could give future export-control policy a cleaner lever against US-China AI dealings. Alongside it, Inkling and Inkling-Small, Thinking Machines' first model family (975B-A41B and 276B-A12B respectively), multimodal MoEs positioned less as benchmark leaders and more as strong bases for downstream fine-tuning.

Tencent's Hy3 (295B-A21B) improved across the board over its predecessor and switched from a restrictive custom license to Apache 2.0 — notable given the prior version's licensing was called out specifically in an earlier Artifacts issue. It also claims to have proven a 50-year-old math problem using a dedicated harness with Sol as judge, though Interconnects flags that the judge's role in that claim is unclear. Poolside's Laguna-S-2.1 (118B-A8B) marks the company's third Artifacts appearance in three months; it fits on a single DGX Spark and ships under the OpenMDW license — an Apache-2.0-like license with AI-specific legal backing — alongside full published evaluation trajectories, an unusual level of transparency for an open release.

Rounding it out: DeepSeek-V4-Flash-0731 landed one day after OpenAI's GPT-5.6 price cuts, reportedly beating Luna at the Pareto frontier — a reminder that in this cycle, model releases and pricing moves are now tightly coupled news events rather than separate stories.

GPT-5.6 Price Cuts Reshape the Cost Curve
Latent Space

OpenAI cut GPT-5.6 pricing across its tiered lineup. Luna, the entry-level model, saw the steepest cut: input dropped from $1 to $0.20 and output from $6 to $1.20 per million tokens — an 80% reduction. Terra, positioned as the daily workhorse model, got a smaller 20% cut to $2/$12. Sol, the flagship, holds its price but adds a new Fast mode running up to 2.5x standard speed at no extra charge — plus cache-write pricing for the first time, while keeping the existing 90% cache-read discount.

The framing that matters here: cost of GPT-5.4-level intelligence has fallen roughly 13x in four months, largely attributed to GPT-5.6's recursive self-optimization. That's a much steeper curve than the usual "prices drop over time" story — it suggests the efficiency gains compound faster than model-generation cycles alone would predict.

The competitive response was immediate: DeepSeek shipped V4-Flash-0731 just one day later, which Interconnects reports beats Luna at the Pareto frontier on price-performance. With capability gaps between frontier labs narrowing, pricing has become the fastest-moving lever labs have left to compete on — expect this tit-for-tat to keep accelerating.
