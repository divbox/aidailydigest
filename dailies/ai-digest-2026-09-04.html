OpenAI's Astra reportedly hides its reasoning from safety monitors
OpenAI's upcoming Astra model reportedly uses "recurrent depth" — looping the same transformer layers repeatedly on a token before answering, squeezing out more capability without adding parameters. OpenAI says it dialed the loops back so Astra still writes out its reasoning, but chief scientist Jakub Pachocki's own framing — wanting to "prevent a race into unmonitorability" — suggests the industry is closer to that race than anyone's comfortable admitting.

Safety & Policy
The Astra "looped transformer" rumor and what it means for monitorability
Latent Space

Reporting from The Information claims OpenAI's upcoming Astra model uses "recurrent depth" — reusing transformer layers in repeated passes over the same input before producing an answer, rather than adding more parameters. Latent Space's recap of Sebastian Raschka's technical breakdown points to an open-weight precedent, Nanbeige 4.2-3B, which reuses a 22-layer stack twice to behave like a 44-layer model without doubling storage — at a cost of roughly 2x compute and only partial retention of standard token-efficiency. The more substantive prior art is Mixture-of-Recursions, where a learned router adaptively decides how many passes each token gets, letting easy tokens exit early.

Raschka's key clarification is one worth sitting with: layer reuse does not automatically imply hidden reasoning. It simply moves more computation into latent activations before a token is emitted. If recurrent depth reduces the visible chain-of-thought a model produces, that's a side effect of needing fewer intermediate tokens to reach an answer — not because looped transformers intrinsically suppress textual reasoning traces.

The actual safety concern is more specific: Astra's extra loop passes reportedly surface as pure math rather than the readable reasoning past models exposed for monitoring purposes, which is the primary tool safety teams currently use to catch a model reasoning its way toward something dangerous. OpenAI says it dialed the loops back so Astra still writes out its reasoning, and its own blog post promises additional monitoring at launch. Chief Scientist Jakub Pachocki has said he wants to "prevent a race into unmonitorability" driven by "confused reporting" — while separately admitting current interpretability tooling is already "fragile," meaning the visibility window into model cognition may be narrowing regardless of what happens with Astra specifically.

Read alongside Anthropic's own admission that it's had to pause its highest-risk RL efforts and bring in METR for independent review of recent agent security incidents, the pattern looks industry-wide: capability is being pushed forward faster than the tooling needed to verify what these systems are actually doing internally.
