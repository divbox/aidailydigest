Qwen3.8-Max: Alibaba's 2.4T open-weight flagship for coding and cowork
Latent Space

Alibaba's Qwen team announced Qwen3.8-Max, a 2.4-trillion-parameter Mixture-of-Experts model pitched as its "most capable model to date," with open weights promised next week alongside a smaller Qwen3.8-27B. API pricing lands at $2.00/M input, $6.00/M output, and $0.25/M cached tokens — a cut from the prior Qwen3.7 Max generation. The launch framing leans hard on long-horizon autonomy: 10+ days of unattended coding, 500+ turns of chip design optimization, and a 365-day e-commerce simulation where the model generated a 4.16x return.

Independent evals back up the hype more than usual for an open-weight release. Qwen3.8-Max debuted #4 on Frontend Code Arena (1,668 Elo, behind only Claude Opus 5 Max and Kimi K3 Max) and #2 on Vision Arena. On the Vals Index it placed #2 among open-weight models and #10 overall at 66.1 — matching Claude Opus 4.7's score at roughly 2.3x lower cost per test. SWE-bench came in at 87.3% (ahead of GPT-5.5 and GLM-5.2, behind Claude Opus 4.8), and Terminal-Bench 2.1 jumped from 61.0 to 67.4 in about 2.5 months of iteration.

The infrastructure reality tempers the pricing story: with roughly 95B active parameters per token (about 4% of total), Qwen3.8-Max still needs at least 8 H100/B200-class GPUs to run — this is not a laptop model. That's part of why the smaller Qwen3.8-27B release may end up mattering more for actual adoption than the flagship itself, since it's the tier most teams can realistically self-host.

The sharpest pushback wasn't about performance but licensing: multiple reports read the terms as restricting use in the US, EU, UK, and Korea, with no clarifying statement from Alibaba in the thread. It's the same tension playing out around MiniMax H3's release this week — "open weights" doesn't necessarily mean unrestricted rights to use, download, or deploy commercially in every jurisdiction.

Zoom out and this is one entry in a broader pattern: Kimi K3, GLM-5.2, DeepSeek V4 Flash, and MiniMax H3 have all landed in the same stretch, and the open-weight frontier has been consistently led by Chinese labs for roughly two years now. Alibaba's decision to open a 2.4T flagship — rather than keeping Max-tier models closed, as it has historically — reads as a bet on ecosystem influence over exclusivity.
