8,100 trials on AI agent skills: they anchor execution, they don't inject knowledge
A multi-university study of 8,135 trial records finds skills work as procedural anchors that stabilize execution, not repositories for missing facts — procedural anchoring accounts for 65.7% of successful cases vs. 4.5% for explicit knowledge injection. Distilling experience into a clean runbook beats raw workflow logs by 6 points, but hiding success/failure annotations during distillation tanks success from 74.6% to 40%. As a skill pool grows from 5 to 100, retrieval precision collapses (29.6% → 3.3%) yet task success holds steady — related "distractor" skills still offer enough partial guidance. Takeaway: write runbooks, annotate outcomes, and gate retrieval by domain buckets.

10% worse, 100x cheaper, 10000x faster: Why Simulation is taking over
Latent Space

Latent Space's Friday essay lays out a single, tidy mental model for the last four years of ML progress: every year since 2022, one more component of the pipeline that produces machine intelligence has flipped from human-made to model-made. What we variously call "synthetic data," "synthetic rubrics," "AI researcher," and "end-to-end RL environments" is, the piece argues, just increasingly ambitious human simulation — 10% worse, but 100x cheaper and 10,000x faster.

The staged history: Stage 1, the reward signal (2022) — InstructGPT's reward model, then Constitutional AI's RLAIF and LLM-as-judge, put the entire approval apparatus on models judging models. Stage 2, the training data (2023) — Phi's "Textbooks Are All You Need," Apple's WRAP rephrasing the web, and Nemotron's synthetic-data pipeline made the corpus substantially model-written. Stage 3, the teacher (2023) — Alpaca, Vicuna, and Orca matured distillation into a discipline, capped by DeepSeek-R1 shipping distilled models alongside the flagship. Stage 4, the curriculum (2024) — Self-Rewarding LMs and SPIN let a model generate its own tasks and improve past its human-preference ceiling.

Then the loop closes on itself. Stage 5, the researcher (2026) — DeepMind's AlphaEvolve and Sakana's AI Scientist, and especially Karpathy's minimal "autoresearch" ratchet loop (700 overnight experiments → 20 kept improvements, cutting time-to-GPT-2 from 2.02 to 1.80 hours). Stage 6, the environment (2026) — Z.ai's GLM-5.3 pipeline synthesizes executable, verifiable task-worlds end to end, with judge agents confirming solvability and verifiers stress-tested until their binary reward is trustworthy. Stage 7, the human subject (2025) — Simile post-trains models on interviews, transaction data, and registered RCTs to recover human bias and inconsistency, turning the focus group, user study, and A/B panel into inference workloads.

Stage 8, the physical world (2026, in progress), is the one row that never fully turns red — and that's the thesis. Poolside's letter draws the line between intelligence-bound problems (soon commoditized) and experiment-bound ones ("100,000 brilliant minds won't cure cancer without a wet lab"); CZ Biohub is compressing the Human Cell Atlas into a virtual cell because in silico is ~1000x cheaper than in vivo. The physical world can only be compressed, cell by cell, into models — never fully synthesized.

The sharpest observation is underneath the grid: every flip was preceded by the same objection — model collapse, hallucination stacking, garbage-in-garbage-out — and every flip happened anyway, at the exact moment a verification mechanism made the synthetic version trustworthy (aggressive filtering, judge-agreement studies, unit tests and proof checkers, oracle/no-op checks, registered RCTs, the wet-lab loop). The synthetic frontier doesn't advance when generation gets better; it advances when verification does. Which points to where it goes next: embodied, physical ground truth is exactly where verification is slowest and most expensive.

The broader signal Latent Space draws: Chinese labs are compressing the frontier on both price/performance and multimodal agents simultaneously, reinforced by OpenAI's same-window 20%+ price cut on GPT-5.6 Sol and SemiAnalysis openly asking whether open models are now catching up.

Business & Industry
Poolside gets a $12B reverse-execuhire to NVIDIA — founders stay, employees go
Latent Space

Less than a month after Latent Space featured Poolside's "Model Factory" with Eiso Kant, NVIDIA moved from investor to licensing Poolside's factory and hiring 109 of its employees — which, per Kant's own framing (fewer than 70 people built the model, ~115 across engineering and research), is the overwhelming majority of the technical team. The founders insist it's "not an acquisition and not an acquihire."

Latent Space coins the structure a "reverse execuhire." In the Windsurf-Google, Character-Google, Scale-Meta, and Instacart-OpenAI deals, executives left with a payout while employees held the remaining company. Here it's inverted: the employees get the comfortable golden parachute (reported figures put employees at ~$6B, founders at ~$1B in the headline), while the founders pivot the company hard toward something new they say they're "not ready to share."

The precipitating cause is nakedly about compute economics. In the founders' telling: "At the end of last year, we had a 6-week window in which to raise $2 billion to pay for a 40,000 GB300 cluster coming online in January. We didn't close it in time, and we lost the cluster." They add that reaching next year's frontier needs far more than an order-of-magnitude-larger cluster, where the binding constraint is "not only capital, it is physical data center space and contracted compute."

The parting thesis is the interesting part, and it dovetails with the "simulation" essay. Poolside splits economically valuable problems into intelligence-bound (building software, accounting, proving theorems — solvable by scaling cognition) and experiment-bound (require real-world experimental feedback loops that no amount of intelligence substitutes for). Their bet: human-level capability gets fully commoditized by open-source models, while superintelligence likely won't, and AI's ultimate, high-margin value comes from becoming "the world's most valuable scientific discovery engine" — where a true data moat derives from real-world experimentation. The PIC infraco spun out in January 2026 hints at where at least one of the three emerging directions is headed.
