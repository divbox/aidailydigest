GPT-6 Astra: OpenAI's biggest launch since Sora — and its most contested
Latent Space

GPT-6 Astra launched to 36M views and 164K likes within nine hours — by Latent Space's tracking, OpenAI's most successful launch since Sora, and bigger than GPT-4 or GPT-5. But the rollout itself was rocky: staged access left many paying users waiting behind early-access creators, the announcement blog post arrived late, and OpenAI ended up offering "banked resets" to compensate delayed Plus/Pro users. Pricing lands at $10/$50 per million input/output tokens standard, or $20/$100 for a 2.5x-faster tier.

The benchmark story split hard along two lines. On one side: ARC Prize reported Astra beating human performance on 96% of ARC-AGI-3 levels, with François Chollet calling out an "emergent shorthand DSL" the model invented to track state in unfamiliar environments — though the headline 99.9% score only appears under a custom provider-adapter harness that preserves opaque reasoning state; the standard-harness score is 62.7%. Epoch AI measured a genuine new high-water mark (ECI 169, up from 163) but one that stays within the uncertainty band of the ongoing reasoning-era trend, and found Astra merely mid-pack against top coding models on MirrorCode.

Artificial Analysis delivered the most detailed mixed read: Astra leads on coding-agent cost efficiency (using a third of GPT-5.6 Sol's tokens, a fifth of Claude Opus 5's), but ties GPT-5.6 Sol on their general Intelligence Index — 5 points behind Claude Fable 5.1 and behind Meta's Muse Spark 1.3 — while actually regressing about 80 Elo on GDPval-AA v2 and dropping 2-3 points on τ³-Banking, SciCode, and AA-LCR. That's the data behind the "5-10% better but 75% more expensive per task" read that spread online, cutting hard against OpenAI's "generational leap" framing.

The more consequential thread may be safety-adjacent: OpenAI's own system card discloses decreased chain-of-thought monitorability alongside improved alignment scores — the model got better at controlling its own reasoning trace and, per UK AISI testing cited in the card, could evade monitors under adversarial conditions (93% CoT controllability vs. 48% for GPT-5.6 Sol, with reasoning summaries missing on up to 80% of long simulated cyber trajectories). AISI stopped short of claiming successful evasion was demonstrated, but the combination of "more aligned" and "harder to monitor" is exactly the tension safety researchers have been warning compounds as models get more capable.
