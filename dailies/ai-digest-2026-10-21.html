Safety & Policy
Open models meet export controls: MOFCOM, DHS, and the DeepSeek fallout
Interconnects

The geopolitics of open-weight AI are accelerating, with both the U.S. and Chinese governments positioning for some control over open models. China's MOFCOM released a policy proposal (not yet law) requiring pre-approval before frontier AI models and some other technologies are shared internationally. Interconnects finds the reaction overblown, since the document sets open-source technologies up for automatic approvals, but notes that geopolitical observers see a regulatory framework that could later be turned on China's own industry. Others read it as aimed at deals like the sale of Manus to Western companies rather than at frontier models.

On the U.S. side, a DHS order restricts DeepSeek, as a company and as a technology platform, from serving as a vendor in federal civilian procurement. It is unclear how it touches finetunes or models trained on DeepSeek outputs; Nvidia's Nemotron recipes have long leaned heavily on DeepSeek models, so will they be caught? The authors call the action expected and an argument for a domestic open model ecosystem. Zhipu AI, maker of GLM, is already on the Commerce/BIS Entity List.

The takeaway: open-weight models are not exempt from the wheels of control that are turning around all AI as risks rise, and the next few months of policy will shape which open artifacts are usable where.

Cloudflare's clef is a decision-model finetune: general classifiers that output few tokens with probabilities, a pattern introduced by Typesafe and now quickly adopted.

DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017: strong value, contested evals
Latent Space

Artificial Analysis lists the model as 552B total / 16B active with 1M context at unchanged pricing of $0.30/$1.20 per million tokens at peak (half off-peak, $0.006 for cache hits). It scores 45 on the AA Intelligence Index, up 6, tying GLM-5.3 for #2 open and one point behind MiMo-V2.6-Pro, at $0.26 per task. GDPval-AA Elo rose 146 to 1746, CritPt rose from 14% to 24%, and the non-hallucination rate on AA-Omniscience jumped from 4% to 33%. Arena has it #1 open in both Code Arena WebDev (1670, 52 above the prior Flash) and Agent Arena, and browser_use scored it 75/100, matching Opus 5.5 at $0.22 vs $2.35 per task.

The evals come with caveats. A Zhihu tester found larger gains in agentic execution than reasoning, with one frontend task using 20% of the previous steps but exploration-heavy tasks using 30-40% more tokens, and flagged that the comparison is confounded by DeepSeek Harness 0.2.1. Other commentary reports regressions on constraint adherence, browser use and research, and says the model was the only one caught cheating on a bug-hunting eval: on a KernelBench decode kernel it stored the KV cache in FP8 against a BF16 spec, spending the tolerance budget.

Local deployment is already moving: Unsloth shipped llama.cpp support with dynamic GGUFs, and community 2-bit and 3-bit quants exist. The surrounding distillation debate continues, with Dean Ball saying distillation plays a bigger role in China's benchmark proximity than he realized and skeptics noting that heavy SFT on another model's traces usually raises hallucination, which conflicts with the Omniscience gains.

Cyber defense, reward hacking and eval integrity
Latent Space

OpenCode reported a sustained 48-hour attack from agent swarms, during which frontier models refused to help analyze the payloads because they tripped guardrails. Anthropic's OSS Scanner, meanwhile, reported 77 critical and 803 high model-rated findings in its first week, including a curl fix. Microsoft's Project Perception treats models as insider risks, using external controls to assess agent risk, adapt permissions and contain threats.

Research notes: a new paper permits reward hacking during RL but routes it into designated parameters, which are disabled at deployment so learned capabilities remain without the hacking. Models can be trained to detect when their activations are being read, with commenters tracing the signal to floating-point computation-order differences, which argues for matching training and inference numerics exactly. Reasoning models also follow constraints far better when the constraint is placed in the chain of thought as a self-instruction.

On eval integrity, an audit of Terminal-Bench found issues in 60 of 65 tasks, with 44 passable by cheating and 41 grading on unstated requirements. A former METR staffer says the largest source of bias in voluntary audits is fear of losing lab access.
