Latent Space

OpenAI attributed a core part of a hidden-reasoning extraction campaign to individuals linked to Moonshot AI: 16,000 attempts from 4,000+ users in two days, with related activity across 15,000+ users. Outside researchers say their attacks kept working on Astra until this week because patches were hard to propagate across product versions and third-party hosts. A related result undercuts current defenses: distillation defenses evaluated without subsequent RL give a false sense of security, since RL makes even simple attacks effective. Apollo Research published principles for embedded evaluators given employee-like lab access.

On cyber, Artificial Analysis's CyberGym-E2E-AA finds some frontier models safety-blocked on 85%+ of tasks, while cheap models like GPT-6 Luna or MiMo-V2.6-Pro can run ~100 bug hunts over a 1M-line codebase for about $20. Anthropic's GLM-5.3 report (50/410 V8 exploits, near Mythos Preview's 56) sharpens the tension: the most capable cheap cyber tool may be an open-weight model with trivially removable refusals, and defenders on r/LocalLlama say that's exactly why they use it.

Provenance got two data points too: SynthID Bio (watermarking designed proteins, in Nature, open-sourced) and a Vals finding that Opus 5.5 and Astra can rewrite more than half a document without Pangram flagging it.

Gemini 4 Argon: strong numbers, cheap tokens, long outputs, limited access
Latent Space

Argon is Google DeepMind's first larger-than-Flash model since Gemini 3.1 Pro in February, arriving after a string of incremental Flash releases and last month's GDM management shakeup. Google claims SOTA on 13 of 19 benchmarks against GPT-6 Astra and Claude Opus 5.5, including 77.9% on DeepSWE (vs 74.2% Opus 5.5, 74.1% Astra). Access starts only with government users and cyber defenders in the Fairwind Program while guardrails are refined.

The genuinely new piece is Long Decode Continuation, an API feature that pauses long responses and resumes them across calls, lifting max output to 1M tokens from 64K. Vals lists a 262K max output; Artificial Analysis reached 1M via the continuation API. Pricing is $4/$20 standard with an open-ended 50% intro discount to $2/$10 and 95% off cached input.

Independent evals are good but less dominant than Google's own: Artificial Analysis puts it at 53 on the Intelligence Index, tied with Astra. Its $1.99 per-task cost beats Astra's $3.26, but the savings come from price, not efficiency, because Argon uses 62K output tokens per task vs Astra's 27K. It leads AutomationBench-AA (77.5%) and the Vals Index (68.9%), and has a striking 15% hallucination rate on AA-Omniscience vs 51% for Astra, though at lower accuracy (50% vs 63%). Terminal-Bench 4 jumped from 19% to ~57% but still trails Sonnet 5.5, Opus 5.5 and Astra.

Google's internal deployment claims are the most interesting signal: Argon agents freed 300+ TiB of data-center memory, are migrating 800K+ lines of C/C++ kernel code to Rust, and replaced 32K lines of SIMD in a video decoder with safe Rust that runs 2.7x faster. Skeptics flagged a weak Harvey legal score (19.6% vs Muse Spark 1.2's 25.42%), possible preference-data benchmaxxing, and disputed DeepSWE figures. With no GA date, its real-world standing stays unproven until people outside Fairwind can use it.

GPT-6.1 Sol and Ultrafast: OpenAI competes on price and speed
Latent Space

OpenAI's DevDay answer to Argon is two-pronged. GPT-6.1 Sol is now #1 on MathArena and #3 on Code Arena WebDev (1759, +70 over GPT-6 Sol at the same $2/$10 pricing). Artificial Analysis measures $0.72 per task at max effort, versus $3.26 for Astra, with savings from fewer turns and a cheaper cache-read price, which is real token efficiency rather than a price cut.

Ultrafast serves Astra at up to ~300 tok/s. SemiAnalysis reports it runs on NVIDIA GPUs at low batch sizes, not Cerebras, so OpenAI is buying latency with utilization. A hands-on tester saw ~8x faster generation but only 2–4x faster end-to-end agent tasks because tool latency dominates; computer use benefits most, since UI actions return in milliseconds. The same tester burned a weekly limit in about two hours.

The product layer matters as much: dots (persistent agents with their own cloud computers), a Decisions API, computer use, and ChatGPT Sites that can host MCP servers as installable plugins. Together with Argon, the theme of the day is long-running agentic work, with Google betting on longer outputs and OpenAI betting on faster ones.

Agents managing themselves: CLMs, adaptive loops, and serving economics
Latent Space

Meta's Context Language Models drop the append-only transcript and treat context as an editable file, with the management policy learned in the weights rather than in a harness. On a 24-hour multi-repo agent-swarm task they score 65% higher at equal compute. Together with Meta's AutoBenchmark, where human input at the ideation stage beats agents alone, it points to more of the agent stack moving into training.

TaH2 uses lookahead depth supervision to teach a looped model which hard tokens deserve another pass: +3.4pp at matched test-time compute and a 53% steeper scaling slope, with a MiniSGL integration that batches requests at different loop depths.

On serving, a steady-state analysis argues prefill/decode disaggregation raises mean interactivity by about 1/(decode-time fraction) at equal batch size and throughput, which helps prefill-heavy workloads but not decode-bound low-latency serving. Hardware notes: a model compiling Triton directly to verified PTX (1.37x on FlashAttention, B200), Cognition first on Vera Rubin at ~4.8x GB200 throughput, and DeepSeek's TileLang toolkit for Ascend 950.
