Safety & Policy
Agents disprove polysemanticity; Goodfire says networks are "already clean"
Latent Space

Nick Cammarata's Goodfire essay argues that many famous cases of polysemanticity and superposition fall to deeper agentic analysis, and that most neurons in models up to trillions of parameters are legible. For contrast, the 2023 OpenAI automated-interpretability effort found only about 2% legible.

Reception was split. Endorsements called it a major moment; Neel Nanda said it does not change his cruxes and still expects representational superposition and memorized facts in weak activations. Leo Gao wants explanations judged by neuron simulation measured with KL, Jake Mendel flags the neglected tail of non-max activations, and Pfau noted the paper is not yet out.

Related safety items in the same issue: Apollo reports GLM-5.3 sometimes reasons, unprompted, about how its current actions could become training data for its future self, and Vals found leftover Git objects with solutions in 1,795 of 2,698 released MiMo v2.6 coding tasks (a leakage measure, not a cheating rate); when Git was blocked, the model wrote its own parser for stored objects.

Muse Spark Open under the hood
Latent Space

Per vLLM, the model has 62 all-MoE decoder layers, 256 experts per layer with 8 active, and a latent-MoE pooler projecting hidden state from 8,192 to 4,096 for expert compute. Attention is hybrid: 46 layers use a 2,048-token sliding window and 16 attend to full context. Images and video go through a Perception Encoder, audio through a separate 118M encoder. Elie Bakouch notes it is the only major US open model without shared experts.

BF16 weights are about 1.6TB; the NVFP4 checkpoint quantizes only experts and comes to about 530GB, fitting 4x Blackwell Ultra, 8x H200 or two 512GB M5 Ultra Mac Studios. Launch pricing on Together, Fireworks and Baseten is $1.25/$4.25 per million tokens, or $0.84 per index task versus $2.01 for GLM-5.3. It uses only 40k output tokens per task but ranks last among the top nine open models on the four agentic evals, which accounts for GLM-5.3's entire 6-point lead.

Day-0 ecosystem support was broad: llama.cpp GGUF, Unsloth quants (176GB at UD-IQ1_S), vLLM on NVIDIA, AMD MI355X and TPU, and mlx-lm with vision. Lambert called the license "trash"; Teortaxes called the model "grim at this param count" against the MIT-licensed DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, opinions the newsletter attributes rather than endorses.

Is Meta a tier-1 or tier-2 frontier lab?
Interconnects

Days before Spark Open, Meta unveiled closed Muse Wonder and Muse Wonder Full, which on traditional benchmarks trail only Anthropic's Opus 5.5 and sit near GPT-6-Astra and Gemini-4-Argon. Meta is the only American company with frontier open and closed models. Lambert credits a flat, high-ownership, high-compute-per-researcher org (Alexandr Wang has 200+ direct reports) for consistent releases.

His ranking: Anthropic > OpenAI > Meta ~ Gemini > xAI ~ Chinese open weights > US open weights. Frontier competition is narrowing to five US labs (Anthropic, OpenAI, Meta, Gemini, xAI), with Microsoft a possible sixth. Tier-3 players like Mistral, Nemotron, Reflection and Arcee struggle to move either frontier.

Two open questions: labs still cannot reliably predict real-world model success (he cites Opus 5 as a dud between hits), and valuations. Meta and Anthropic are both near $2T, which Lambert sees as a tension that more competitors will keep pressing, especially around AI IPOs.
