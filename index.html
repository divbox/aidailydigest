DeepInfer: production inference, open-source boundaries, and cached-token economics
Latent Space

DeepInfer is DeepSeek's customization of vLLM (with credit to SGLang and TensorRT-LLM) and the engine behind V4.1 Flash. It separates vision encoding, prefill and decode, uses wide expert parallelism for decode, and schedules prefill by estimated attention compute rather than raw request length. Decode routing balances both running-request count and KV length, and expert replicas are periodically redistributed to counter routing skew. The disclosed Blackwell setup appears to avoid tensor parallelism entirely, a choice commentators trace to H800-era interconnect limits.

The cache design is the key to the headline numbers. Persistent block KV sits in a roughly 100 PiB 3FS SSD cluster with reported retention of three days for agents and seven for chatbots, so any prefill worker can fetch a prefix. SWA Bounded Replay rebuilds short-lived sliding-window state from only the latest 128 tokens, which DeepSeek says nearly halves prefill compute. So 155T tokens/day with a 96% input cache hit is not 155T newly computed tokens. The KV cache management and load balancer are not released.

On economics, $38 revenue per accelerator-hour at off-peak prices implies roughly 74-87% accelerator-only margins at $5-10/hour GPU cost, but that omits the SSD fleet, networking, idle capacity and engineering. One widely shared estimate had to be corrected from 88-97% to 84-96%. Latent Space's own math puts it above 80%. vLLM answered by reporting decode throughput within 6% of DeepInfer on GB200 in a matched configuration. That is decode-stage parity, not reproduction of the whole service.

Hardware: Ascend support is confirmed, but Ascend production efficiency is not. One analysis cites about 25.5K versus 5K tokens/s/device at 100 TPS for GB200 versus Ascend 950DT, combining different measurements and assumed prices. Treat it as directional.

Scientific agents: wet-lab results and open-ended limits
Latent Space

The scorpion-toxin work yielded 17 binders from 211 tested designs, with all designs and lab data being released. None bound multiple toxins and none were tested for neutralization, so cellular and animal validation remain ahead.

Sophont's Holon-S1 predicts spatial gene activity and biomarkers from routine pathology slides, evaluated across 16 organ types and 15+ scanners; these are research findings rather than validated treatment-selection outcomes.

The counterweight: evaluators gave GPT-6 Astra and Claude Fable 5.1 unpublished AI-research questions and $10,000 budgets each. Original paper authors saw better experimental design and resource management but still rejected both papers over creativity, judgment, calibration and communication.
