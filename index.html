The AI race will extend far beyond Earth
Import AI

AI Frontiers argues the 2030s space economy (orbital power, compute and manufacturing, enabled by Starship-class lift) will be shaped by AI. Autonomy and redesign: spacecraft can operate on their own and be designed around hosting AIs rather than humans, so a debris strike that would doom a crewed station might leave an uncrewed, unpressurized vessel largely functional.

The strategic worry is compounding first-mover advantage: an industrial flywheel where infrastructure begets cheaper energy and manufacturing, strong domain awareness that could deny access to others, and self-replicating probes expanding into deep space.

Clark agrees and expects space to move from academic topic to practical relevance quickly. Given distance and communication latency, he expects a good share of solar-system activity to become dominated by autonomous machines.

Cross-lab federated training at US national labs
Import AI

Two DOE projects show how the government might pool compute. In the tri-lab NNSA effort, Sandia, LLNL and LANL trained a LLaMA-based model across El Capitan (AMD MI300A) and H100 machines at SNL and LANL, keeping access-restricted data at its originating site. Training used torchtitan, with NVFlare, Flower and APPFL compared on LLaMA 3.x at 1B, 8B and 70B scales. The authors say the workflow is production-ready and has run on larger classified machines.

The ASCR effort federated fine-tuning of LLaMA-2 7B on the SMolInstruct chemistry dataset across roughly 1,700 Intel, NVIDIA and AMD GPUs on Aurora, Polaris, Frontier and Perlmutter.

Clark's caveat: the scale is about 100x short of frontier. To operate near parity with industry, governments would need to link ~100k chips. Still, he calls it an important start.

TasteVal: measuring AI research taste
Import AI

P-Zero Research's TasteVal gives a model eight tasks representative of frontier development (pretraining data curation, pretraining and fine-tuning, preference modeling, adversarial robustness) and measures how well it iteratively designs experiments and draws conclusions. Results are compared to a baseline from 24 experts recently at OpenAI, Google DeepMind, NVIDIA and Microsoft Research.

The headline: experimental research taste has doubled every 3.0 months since December 2025 (95% CI 1.7-5.0). The best model, Opus 5.5, beats the human baseline with a 2.30x compute multiplier (CI 1.15-4.37x) at roughly 1/30 the cost per attempt. Progress was much slower in prior years, with the slope changing around the turn of 2026.

Clark ties this to his earlier recursive self-improvement analysis, where creativity was the key variable. With creative math breakthroughs and now this benchmark, he moves his estimate for full RSI from 2028 to 2027.

Humanity's Sixth Sense (HSS)
Import AI

Elorian and Scale AI built HSS to test how models reason about what a scene implies but doesn't show, such as whether a bookcase can fit more books or whether chains bear a plough's drag. It has 522 questions over 288 images and 234 video clips (17.6 hours), across temporal and causal dynamics, physical and spatial logic, social understanding, and abstract inference.

Humans score 93.1 pass@1. The best model, GPT 6 Astra, scores 53.6 (image 55.6, video 51.1, time 58.2, space 53.6). Chinese open-weight models do poorly: GLM-5.3-flash 26.8, Kimi K3 25.5. The top failure modes are missing a decisive detail (21%) and misidentifying an object, person or role (20%).

Clark's reading: the decent showing of general LLMs counters the claim that visual reasoning requires dedicated spatially aware 2D/3D world models, which suggests such models may be emergent.

Reflection's Beam: token-efficient, but behind Chinese open models
Import AI

Reflection's Beam is a 501B-parameter MoE with 23B active, pretrained on 23.8T tokens using 6,144 GB300 NVL72 GPUs for four weeks, then RL on ~10.5K of the same GPUs for four more. The RL run drew on one million coding, agentic and STEM environments, which Reflection believes is among the largest open-lab RL runs to date.

Scores: DeepSWE 1.1 44.4 (GLM-5.3: 61), GPQA Diamond 90.5 (91.7), MCP Atlas 78.7 (84.2). The pitch is efficiency: comparable to GLM-5.2 on advanced reasoning while using 3-4x less inference compute. Weights, technical report and model card are promised later this month.

Clark sees a chance for the US to return to the open-weight frontier, which it largely left when Meta stopped producing strong LLaMA models; only Google's small Gemma line remains competitive.

Import AI

Mistral announced Large 4 (ML4), with open weights due by the end of the month. It was trained on 3,800 NVIDIA Grace Blackwell GPUs in European datacenters and is marketed on top-tier cyber performance with fewer refusals than proprietary Anthropic and OpenAI models, plus self-deployment under an organization's own policies.

Clark calls Mistral Europe's best hope for a national champion, but says the results show how hard it is to compete with China.
