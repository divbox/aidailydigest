Models & Research
Open model roundup: Motif-3, GLM-5.3, Hy4-preview, and a widening licensing split
Interconnects

Interconnects has been tracking open model licenses for a while, and the history matters for reading this week's news: 2025 was full of custom licenses — Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct, the Llama licenses, and DeepSeek's custom V3 license before DeepSeek switched to MIT with R1. That MIT move helped push many Chinese model makers toward MIT or Apache 2.0 licenses through the rest of 2025.

2026 has split that trend in two. Western labs are opening up — both Google and Meta have moved to Apache 2.0 — while frontier Chinese labs are getting more restrictive. Kimi K3's license requires commercial agreements for anyone running inference or fine-tuning services on it. MiniMax M3 requires agreements above a revenue threshold and bans certain use cases outright. The newest addition is Zhipu's GLM-5.3, which dropped the MIT license used by GLM-5.2 and earlier in favor of a custom license: if a licensee and its affiliates clear $10 billion in aggregate revenue over any trailing 12 months, they must pass Z.AI's security review before any commercial use. The $10B bar is unusually high as these clauses go, but "affiliates" is left undefined in the English text, and the Chinese version uses a term (关联方) that does carry a specific legal definition — creating real ambiguity for anyone trying to assess exposure.

On the model side, a few releases stood out. Motif-3 (MIT-licensed, from Motif Technologies) continues an impressive trajectory for a small team working with limited resources. dots3-note-prev, from RedNote/Xiaohongshu, reportedly won IMO 2026 with a perfect score using an internal harness. Qwen3.8-Flash-Next previews Qwen's next architecture generation — a 125B-A6B mixture-of-experts design using GDN and Qwen Sparse Attention, continuing the direction set by Qwen3-Next-80B-A3B-Instruct. GLM-5.3-Flash is notable less for its architecture than its go-to-market: it was quietly released as a "stealth model" under the name "Ox-Alpha" on OpenRouter and OpenCode, letting speculation about its size and origin (guesses ranged from a >1T Cursor/xAI model to a new Gemini) build hype before Zhipu revealed it — a playbook that also blunts benchmaxxing accusations. Hy4-preview from Tencent is competent but still has an overthinking problem; if its trajectory mirrors the jump from Hy3-preview to Hy3, the final version could be a legitimate contender among open models.

The through-line is that "open" is no longer a single category. License fragmentation among the Chinese frontier labs — especially clauses with undefined terms like GLM-5.3's — creates real compliance uncertainty for companies trying to build on these models commercially, even as the raw capability of open releases keeps climbing. Whether that friction pushes developers toward the genuinely permissive options (Apache 2.0, MIT) or turns out to be mostly unenforceable boilerplate will depend on how the first real disputes over these clauses play out.
